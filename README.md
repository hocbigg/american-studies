## Introduction

American Studies is an interdisciplinary field that investigates the culture, history, and society of the United States. Rather than analyzing historical events, literary works, or political structures in isolation, it asks how these domains intersect. It looks at how national mythologies, such as the "American Dream", the frontier thesis, and American exceptionalism, were created and challenged, and how diverse populations have negotiated identity, power, and belonging within and across U.S. borders.

This curriculum is for independent learners starting from scratch. It assumes no background in literary criticism, historiography, or political science. Whether you are an independent scholar, a career changer, or a generalist who wants a grounded cultural understanding of the United States, it gives you an undergraduate-level foundation using openly accessible textbooks, university lecture series, and seminal texts.

You can study each subject on its own, but a deliberate order helps you build a cohesive analytical framework:

1. Start with *Introduction to American Studies*. It sets out the field's theoretical vocabulary, including cultural critique, myth-and-symbol analysis, and transnational perspectives, which you will use to question everything that follows.
2. Study the *American History Survey* and the *American Literature Survey* together if you can. Novels, poetry, and political tracts both come from and respond to their historical circumstances, so pairing chronological history with the matching literary periods lets each illuminate the other.
3. Move on to *American Government and Political Culture* for the institutional architecture, constitutional debates, and civic mechanisms that govern American public life.
4. Finish with *Themes in American Culture and Society*, where history, literature, politics, and sociology come together on recurring debates about race, gender, class, regionalism, and mass media.

This page covers the foundational core: the main chronologies, constitutional frameworks, literary movements, and interpretive methods that every American Studies student needs before specializing. It does not cover every subdiscipline or niche research method.

Once you have this foundation, [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md) covers subfields such as settler colonialism, queer and feminist theory, transnational empire, and visual culture.

## Curriculum

### Introduction to American Studies

This subject covers the intellectual history, core concepts, and interdisciplinary methods of American Studies, including how scholars analyze national myths, cultural identity, and transnational connections.

[The Myths That Made America: An Introduction to American Studies (Heike Paul / OAPEN Library)](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/31456) - An open-access, peer-reviewed textbook that introduces the field by analyzing foundational national myths, including the Promised Land, the frontier, and the self-made individual.

[American Studies: A User's Guide (Philip J. Deloria & Alexander I. Olson / University of California Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780520287730) - A methodology handbook with practical tools for interpreting cultural texts, curating archives, and analyzing power dynamics across the discipline.

[American Studies: The Basics (Andrew Dix / Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781138775046) - A shorter alternative to the Paul and Deloria texts that summarizes the field's historical development and central academic debates.

### American History Survey

This subject traces the social, political, and cultural development of the United States in chronological order, from pre-Columbian contact to the modern era.

[The American Yawp: A Massively Collaborative Open U.S. History Textbook (Stanford University Press)](https://www.americanyawp.com/) - A collaboratively written open textbook that gives priority to social, cultural, and marginalized perspectives, which makes it a good narrative backbone for an American Studies learner.

[U.S. History (OpenStax)](https://openstax.org/details/books/us-history) - An open-access alternative with a more traditional political and chronological overview.

[History of the United States I & II (Modern States)](https://modernstates.org/course/history-of-the-united-states-i/) - A two-part video lecture sequence running from early colonization to the present, for learners who would rather watch than read a survey textbook.

[The American Revolution  —  HIST 116 (Yale Open Courses / Joanne Freeman)](https://oyc.yale.edu/history/hist-116) - A 25-lecture deep dive meant to be watched alongside the Revolutionary era chapters of your survey textbook.

[The Civil War and Reconstruction Era, 1845–1877  —  HIST 119 (Yale Open Courses / David Blight)](https://oyc.yale.edu/history/hist-119) - Builds on your survey studies with detailed historical and cultural analysis of the sectional crisis, slavery, and Reconstruction.

### American Literature Survey

This subject surveys the major literary movements, authors, and aesthetics that have reflected and challenged American identity from the colonial era to the present.

[American Passages: A Literary Survey (Annenberg Learner)](https://www.learner.org/series/american-passages-a-literary-survey/) - A 16-part video course that places literary movements, canonical figures, and recovered writers in their historical and cultural moments.

[American Literature (Modern States)](https://modernstates.org/course/american-literature/) - A short lecture series aligned with standard survey exams, useful for a quick review of the major literary periods.

[The Norton Anthology of American Literature, Shorter Tenth Edition (Robert S. Levine et al. / W. W. Norton)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780393892291) - The standard two-volume primary-text reader, to be read in order alongside the video courses, with contextual introductions and author biographies.

### American Government and Political Culture

This subject covers the constitutional foundations, governing institutions, civil rights, and civic cultures of the American political system.

[American Government 4e (OpenStax)](https://openstax.org/details/books/american-government-4e) - An open-access textbook on constitutional design, institutional powers, political behavior, and civil liberties.

[American Government (Modern States)](https://modernstates.org/course/american-government/) - A self-paced video course on foundational civics and constitutional mechanics, and a video alternative to the textbook.

[Democracy in America (Annenberg Learner)](https://www.learner.org/series/democracy-in-america/) - A 15-part video series that uses contemporary case studies and Tocquevillian theory to show how democratic institutions work in practice.

[The Federalist Papers (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1404) - A collection of primary political essays to read selectively alongside the institutional coursework, for a view of constitutional design and early political thought.

### Themes in American Culture and Society

This subject explores the main cultural arenas, recurring tensions, and contested identities that define American life, including race, gender, class, regionalism, and popular culture.

[American Cultural Studies: An Introduction to American Culture, Fourth Edition (Neil Campbell & Alasdair Kean / Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781138833142) - The standard thematic textbook for the discipline, with chapters on the American landscape, urbanization, ethnicity, religion, mass media, and gender.

[Keywords for American Cultural Studies, Third Edition (NYU Press / Keywords Project)](https://keywords.nyupress.org/american-cultural-studies/) - A collection of open-access scholarly essays on key critical concepts such as citizenship, border, empire, and race, to read alongside your thematic reading.
